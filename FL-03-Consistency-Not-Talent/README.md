# FL-03 — Consistency, Not Talent

## Assignment Overview
This assignment focuses on judgment, not raw design talent. The goal is to build a visual system that makes the portfolio feel intentional, clear, and credible without distracting from the actual proof.

The core principle is:

The design frames the work. It never upstages it.

This week is about learning how to choose strong visual decisions instead of chasing flashy effects.

---

## Core Principle
A portfolio should make the work feel stronger, not louder.

That means:
- design supports the story
- typography is readable and deliberate
- use of motion is minimal and purposeful
- background treatments stay quiet
- case studies remain the primary visual focus

The portfolio should feel like a professional presentation of proof, not an aesthetic experiment.

---

## Rule Against AI Slop
The assignment also introduces a second filter: avoid the generic “AI slop” look.

This includes:
- fake glassmorphism
- melted or glossy effects
- too much blur
- excessive glow
- overcomplicated backgrounds
- busy patterns that compete with the content
- motion that is more memorable than the actual work

AI-generated visuals are acceptable only when they serve the content and remain subtle. They should never become the main attraction.

## Image Strategy
The portfolio should rely mostly on real proof.

### Use real captures for:
- personal photo
- dental case documentation
- dashboard screenshots
- project UI screenshots
- real app flows and product images

### Use AI only for:
- abstract process diagrams
- subtle geometric background textures
- soft atmospheric visuals that do not pretend to be product proof

Never generate fake screenshots of real software experiences or fake clinical outcomes.

## Kill Your Darlings: Image Judgment
This is the most important part of the assignment. The skill is not in generating many images; it is in rejecting the wrong ones.

### A) Process / architecture diagram
#### Concept 1: Minimal system map
Keep.

Why: it is readable, structured, and professional. It helps explain the system without distracting from the portfolio’s core proof.

#### Concept 2: Glassmorphism cloud diagram
Reject.

Why: it falls into fake-glass AI slop. The blur, glow, and translucent layers compete with the real work and make the portfolio feel synthetic.

#### Concept 3: Dense futuristic network diagram
Reject.

Why: it is too busy, too noisy, and too decorative. It communicates visual intensity instead of clarity, which breaks the “frame, not upstage” rule.

### B) CTA background texture
#### Concept 1: Soft botanical abstraction
Keep.

Why: subtle, calm, and aligned to the brand. It creates mood without reducing legibility or stealing attention from the message.

#### Concept 2: Melted glass blob background
Reject.

Why: it feels artificial and generic. The fake depth and glossy shapes compete with the CTA text and look like decorative AI noise.

#### Concept 3: Busy motion field
Reject.

Why: the visual energy is louder than the message itself. It competes with the CTA and breaks the professional focus of the portfolio.

The strongest portfolio identity is:

- real proof first
- subtle supporting visuals only

The portfolio should feel credible, not trendy.

The design frames the work. It never upstages it.
